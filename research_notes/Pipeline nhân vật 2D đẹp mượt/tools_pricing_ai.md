# Tools, 2026 pricing, licences and capability limits for a commercial-quality animated 2D-looking game character (incl. AI tools)

Research date: 2026-11-21. Method note for the report writer: this session's network blocked direct fetches of most vendor sites (esotericsoftware.com, live2d.com, cascadeur.com, aseprite.org, copyright.gov, steampowered.com, meshy.ai and others returned DNS errors). Figures below come from (a) web-search summaries of official pages and reputable outlets and (b) licence/README files fetched directly from GitHub/Hugging Face (raw.githubusercontent.com, huggingface.co), which did work. Where only third-party or conflicting figures exist, this is flagged. Before publishing, re-check every price on the vendor page. Target use case: a small team, top-down/45° action RPG, winged armoured knight with greatsword, many attacks, 8 directions, starting from AI-generated 2D concept sheets, with a browser/canvas prototype.

## 1. 2D animation tools (Spine, Live2D Cubism, DragonBones, Creature, Moho, Toon Boom Harmony, Aseprite)

### Takeaway
For a canvas/web game with 8 directions and many attacks, the realistic 2D options are Spine (skeletal, mature web runtimes, one-time price, but a $500k revenue cap before Enterprise) or frame-by-frame/pre-rendered sprites edited in Aseprite ($19.99). Live2D targets face/upper-body "breathing" illustration (VTubers, gacha portraits) rather than 8-direction top-down combat. DragonBones is effectively legacy and now points to "LoongBones". Moho and Toon Boom are animation-production tools whose output must be baked to frames for a game.

### Inferences
- For a winged knight with cape, wings and greatsword trails, Spine Professional is the minimum Spine tier (meshes and weights). At under $500k/yr revenue a small team pays only per-seat one-time fees; crossing $500k (including VC money) forces Enterprise at about $2.5k/yr.
- 8 directions in skeletal 2D is expensive: typically 5 unique views (S, SE, E, NE, N) mirrored to 8, each needing its own art cut-up and animation set. The many-attacks requirement multiplies this. That is why many top-down ARPGs either pre-render 3D to sprites (Diablo/Hades-like) or use 4–5 directions.
- Live2D is the wrong tool for gameplay animation here; it is only useful for dialogue portraits.

## 2. 3D modelling/texturing tools (Blender, Maya/3ds Max Indie, ZBrush, Substance 3D Painter, 3DCoat)

### Takeaway
Blender (free, GPL; outputs are yours) plus a cheap texturing tool covers the "3D-to-2D pre-render" route. Autodesk Indie is about $305/yr but capped at <$100k revenue and project value <$100k (country-dependent). ZBrush is now Maxon subscription-only (iPad free tier; paid about $9.99/mo or $89.99/yr for the iPad tier; desktop pricing not confirmed). Substance 3D Painter is still sold as a Steam perpetual licence (2026 version about C$259.99 / A$289.95) with team-use ambiguity. 3DCoat perpetual rose from €119 to €159 for 2026 individuals.

## 3. Rigging, animation and motion (Mixamo, Auto-Rig Pro, Rigify, AccuRIG/ActorCore, Cascadeur, mocap, marketplace sword packs)

### Takeaway
Auto-riggers (Mixamo, AccuRIG) handle the humanoid body but officially not wings. The standard workaround is to rig the biped, then add wing bones manually in Blender (Auto-Rig Pro $50 helps, with Unity/Unreal/Godot export). Cascadeur (AI-assisted physics posing) is free for non-commercial use only; Indie is for <$100k/yr revenue. Greatsword combat is well served by Unreal/Unity marketplace packs (95–205 clips) retargetable to a custom skeleton, then rendered to 8-direction sprites.

## 4. AI image-to-3D in 2026 (Tripo, Meshy, Rodin/Hyper3D, Hunyuan3D, TRELLIS/TRELLIS.2, SF3D/SPAR3D, Sudo AI, CSM) and why TripoSR looked poor

### Takeaway
Commercial tools (Tripo 3.x, Meshy 6/7, Rodin Gen-2/2.5) now offer multi-view input, quad or low-poly remesh, PBR textures and auto-rigging for about $20–$120/mo, with commercial rights only on paid tiers (Meshy free = CC BY 4.0). Open weights: TRELLIS / TRELLIS.2 (MIT, 4B, PBR, up to 1536³, needs ≥24 GB GPU) are the most permissive; Hunyuan3D 2.x is open but its licence excludes the EU, UK and South Korea and requires a separate licence above 1M MAU (2.5/3.x reportedly closed/API-only). Outputs are rarely truly game-ready for a hero character: expect retopo/cleanup, especially for wings, armour plates and the sword (separate parts). TripoSR (Mar 2024) was a fast single-image triplane reconstructor (marching cubes at 256 resolution by default, vertex colours), which explains blobby geometry and smeared backs versus 2025–26 native-3D diffusion models.

### Inferences
- Why TripoSR looked poor (synthesised from the README facts above): (1) one image only, so the back and sides are hallucinated; (2) triplane plus marching cubes at 256³, so soft, blobby geometry with no sharp armour edges, and stair-stepping; (3) vertex colours instead of a UV texture, so low texture resolution and baked lighting; (4) a dense, unstructured triangle soup with no topology for rigging; (5) deterministic regression averages ambiguity into mush instead of sampling a plausible sharp shape. 2025–26 tools add generative 3D latent diffusion (TRELLIS O-Voxel up to 1536³, Hunyuan DiT), separate high-res PBR texture models, multi-view conditioning (Meshy 1–4 images, Hunyuan3D-2mv, Tripo multi-view), and remesh/quad/low-poly stages.
- For this knight: feed front, side and back turnaround views (multi-view), generate body, sword and possibly wings as separate parts (Tripo "Generate Parts"), then retopo in Blender/3DCoat (AI quad output is a starting point), then rig. Thin wing membranes or feathers and the greatsword blade are classic failure points (thickness, fused geometry). Model the sword manually or simplify it.
- For a 2D-looking final, mesh topology matters less if the 3D model is only used to render sprites. The cleanup priority is silhouette, deformation at shoulders, wing roots and hips, and texture sharpness at the target sprite size.
- Licensing choice: if any team member, publisher or market is in the EU/UK/Korea, avoid self-hosted Hunyuan3D open weights; prefer TRELLIS.2 (MIT) or paid Tripo/Meshy/Rodin plans. Never ship Meshy free-tier output without attribution (CC BY 4.0) or Tripo free output at all (non-commercial).

## 6. Legal: copyright of AI-generated assets (US Copyright Office 2025) and Steam AI disclosure

### Takeaway
The US Copyright Office (Part 2 report, Jan 29, 2025) says prompts alone, however detailed, do not make the user the author. Purely AI-generated elements are not copyrightable, but human-authored selection, arrangement, modification, and human work perceptible in the output can be protected case by case. Steam (rules revised in January 2026) requires disclosure of AI content that players consume (pre-generated art, audio, text) and guardrails for live-generated content; behind-the-scenes efficiency tools are exempt. Implication: use AI as a base and add documented human authorship (repaint, retopo, animation, composition), keep prompt/edit logs, and disclose on Steam.

### Cited Findings
- USCO released "Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability" on January 29, 2025 — [USCO NewsNet 1060](https://copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html). Prompts alone do not provide sufficient human control to make users authors; human-authored works perceptible in outputs, and creative selection, arrangement or modification, can be protected; using AI as an assistant or including AI material in a larger human work does not bar copyright; case-by-case; no new legislation needed — [Library of Congress blog](https://blogs.loc.gov/copyright/2025/02/inside-the-copyright-offices-report-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence-part-2-copyrightability/); [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/01/29/part-two-copyright-office-ai-report-says-creative-prompting-doesnt-constitute-authorship/id=185455/); [Holland & Knight](https://hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/01/us-copyright-office-issues-report-on-copyrightability-of-generative-ai); [Skadden](https://skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/02/copyright-office-publishes-report).

### Inferences
